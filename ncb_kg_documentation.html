This document describes the ontology model used by the knowledge graph extracted from the NCB News Bulletin corpus. It follows the structure of the published DTO documentation, with one addition: because this documents a populated graph rather than a schema alone, every term carries the number of instances asserted against it.

SECTION 1Introduction

The graph is built against two namespaces. Terms in dto: are the published Drug Trafficking Ontology, used unmodified. Terms in dtox: are an additive extension introduced here, because published DTO declares no datatype properties and so cannot express a quantity, a date, a monetary amount or a place name.

@prefix dto: <https://w3id.org/def/DTO/> . @prefix dtox: <https://w3id.org/def/DTO/ext#> . @prefix kg: <https://w3id.org/def/DTO/kg/> .
Namespace caveat

The dtox: namespace is not yet registered or resolvable. It was chosen to sit naturally beneath the published DTO namespace and must be replaced before publication.

SECTION 6Drug taxonomy

Substances named in the press are mapped onto the UNODC IDS controlled vocabulary, so text-derived drugs and UNODC-derived drugs are the same classes and can be joined. Indian street names are handled: ganja → cannabis herb, charas → cannabis resin, MD → mephedrone, yaba → methamphetamine, brown sugar and smack → heroin.

SECTION 8Provenance

Every assertion is traceable. Each extracted event carries dtox:reportedIn pointing at the article it came from, and each article carries its original publisher URL on dtox:sourceURL plus the bulletin it appeared in. No assertion in the graph is free-floating.

SECTION 9Known limitations

Extraction is rule-based — recall is a lower bound

Entities are found by gazetteer and regular expression, not a trained model. Every extraction is traceable to a pattern you can read, but coverage is incomplete: of 1,397 press seizure events only 719 carry a quantity.

That gap is deliberate. A quantity is attached only when an article names exactly one substance. Where a report mentions both heroin and ganja there is no reliable way to know which figure belongs to which, so nothing is attached rather than guessing.

Incident vs aggregate reporting

A news article may describe one incident or a period total (“seized since 2024”, a drug-destruction ceremony, an annual report). Summing across the two double-counts. Every event therefore carries dtox:hasReportScope, either dtox:IncidentLevel (1,085 seizures) or dtox:AggregateLevel (312). Quantities and values are attached only at incident level.
